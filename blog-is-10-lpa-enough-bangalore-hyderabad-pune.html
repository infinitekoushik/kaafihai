Is 10 LPA enough in Bangalore, Hyderabad and Pune?

Updated November 2026

A 10 LPA offer sounds like a clear yes until you ask where you will live. This post runs the same offer through Bangalore, Hyderabad and Pune using the Kaafi Hai cost model, for a single person. All figures are estimates, not survey data.

10 LPA is not ₹83,333 in your account

Divide 10,00,000 by 12 and you get ₹83,333. That is the most you could see each month, and only if the whole CTC is paid out as monthly cash. CTC can also include employer PF, gratuity, insurance and variable pay, so the in-hand figure is often lower. Your offer letter or salary slip shows the real number, so ask for it.

Below, three in-hand levels are used: ₹65,000, ₹75,000 and ₹83,333. They are examples to show the range, not what any employer pays. Income tax is left out because under the new regime for FY 2026-27 a salaried person with ₹10 lakh of pay owes none. The ₹75,000 standard deduction brings taxable income to ₹9,25,000, which is under the ₹12 lakh limit for the Section 87A rebate. The old regime works differently.

The calculator calls anything under 10% kept tight, and Bangalore lands there. At ₹75,000 in hand the comfortable tier in Bangalore costs ₹2,300 more than you take home. Hyderabad and Pune still keep about a fifth.

So is 10 LPA enough?

For one person at a moderate lifestyle, yes in all three cities, with room to save in each. The gap between cities is real, though. Bangalore stops working on 10 LPA once you want a comfortable lifestyle. If a Bangalore offer pays more, the question is whether the raise covers ₹7,200 a month, and the calculator tests that for your role.

What these numbers leave out

The model assumes one person, no EMIs, no money sent home and no savings target. Rent is a city-level estimate, and a flat in Koramangala is not a flat in Electronic City. If any of that applies to you, put your own figures into the calculator, which also lets you set household size.
