When does Mumbai or Bangalore beat Hyderabad on take-home?

Updated November 2026

A bigger city costs more and usually pays more. Whether it leaves you with more each month depends on one number: the pay in your current city above which the higher salary covers the higher cost. This post works that number out for Bangalore, Mumbai, Delhi and Pune against Hyderabad, using the Kaafi Hai model. Everything here is an estimate for one person at a moderate lifestyle, not survey data.

Step 2: how much extra each city pays

The premium is not the same for every job. Mumbai pays finance roles much more than it pays teachers, and Bangalore leans towards tech. These are the premiums over Hyderabad in the model.

Step 3: the break-even salary

Divide the extra cost by the premium and you get the pay in Hyderabad at which the other city leaves exactly the same amount. Above it, the bigger city leaves more. Below it, you keep more by staying put.

Read it by role category. For a tech role, Mumbai only wins above ₹1,22,500 a month in Hyderabad, while a finance role crosses over at ₹58,800. For engineering, teaching and similar roles, Mumbai needs ₹1,83,800, which is above every salary the model uses for those roles.

A mid-level Software Engineer keeps ₹2,700 less in Mumbai than in Hyderabad, even though the Mumbai salary is ₹12,000 higher. A Finance Analyst keeps ₹4,050 more. A teacher keeps ₹11,100 less, because a small premium cannot cover Mumbai rents.

What this leaves out

The model uses one premium per role category and city, so a specific company can pay far above or below it. It also ignores moving costs, deposits, bonuses and tax, and it assumes one person at a moderate lifestyle.
