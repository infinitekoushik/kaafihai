How to compare two job offers in different cities

Updated November 2026

A higher salary in a more expensive city is not automatically a better offer. This is a short method for comparing two offers fairly, with one worked example. The living-cost figures come from the calculator and are estimates.

1. Put both offers on the same basis

Use monthly fixed pay for both. Leave out bonuses that are not guaranteed, and check whether each figure is CTC or what reaches your account. Comparing a CTC with an in-hand number is the most common mistake.

2. Subtract living costs for the same lifestyle

Pick one lifestyle level and apply it to both cities. Rent is the biggest line, so it drives most of the gap. In our estimates, moderate rent is ₹17,000 in Hyderabad and ₹22,000 in Bangalore.

3. Compare the rupee gap and the share kept

Take what is left each month in each city and also divide it by the salary. The first number tells you the surplus, the second tells you how much of your pay survives the basics.

A worked example

Say you have two offers for the same software engineer role: Hyderabad at ₹1,00,000 a month in Hyderabad, and Bangalore at ₹1,15,000 a month in Bangalore. Both are moderate lifestyle, no tax deducted.

OfferSalaryLiving costLeftShare kept
Hyderabad₹1,00,000₹32,300₹67,70068%
Bangalore₹1,15,000₹39,500₹75,50066%

The Bangalore offer leaves ₹7,800 more each month, but it keeps 66% of the salary against 68% for Hyderabad. Whether the extra ₹7,800 is worth the higher cost base is a personal call. The point is to see both numbers before deciding.

4. Add what the numbers leave out

Check one-time costs such as moving and a rental deposit, which in many cities is several months of rent. Ask about relocation support, joining bonus and notice-period buyout. Think about commute time, because a cheaper area far from the office costs you hours. Income tax and deductions apply to both offers and shrink the surplus in each.

5. Decide what you are optimising for

If you want the biggest monthly surplus, look at rupees left. If you want a lower-risk base, look at the share kept. If you are early in your career, learning and the quality of the team can matter more than either.
